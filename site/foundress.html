Foundress of the Satyaseva Sisters · 1921 – 2012

Rev. Mother Regina Woroniecka

An orphan from Poland who became a teacher, artist, and missionary — who walked the roads of Karnataka to bring the faith into the homes of the poor.

Born
12 September 1921 · Bydgoszcz, Poland
Congregation founded
1976 · Diocese of Mysore, Karnataka
Died
23 September 2012 · Poznań-Pokrzywno, Poland
1921 – 1943 Bydgoszcz · Poznań · Warsaw

An orphan finds a school

Regina was born in Bydgoszcz on 12 September 1921. She lost both parents when she was eight, and her guardians placed her in the Ursuline boarding school in Poznań.

There she completed her primary and secondary education, passing her matura exam in Warsaw in August 1940. She would later write that she was always grateful for the school’s atmosphere of faith and its respect for each girl’s individuality.

1943 – 1969 Kraków · Tarnów · Poznań · Lublin

Sister Jolanta — teacher and artist

In 1943 she asked to join the Ursulines of the Roman Union, formed in the spirituality of St Angela Merici. She entered the novitiate in Kraków in 1944 and took the name Jolanta. She made her first vows in 1946 and her final profession in Lublin in 1949.

Within the convent she studied Roman philology, archaeology and theology, spending a year at the Academy of Fine Arts in Kraków. Her superiors gave her work in education: she formed candidates in Tarnów, taught in Kraków and Poznań, and ran a dormitory in Lublin, where she was prioress from 1965.

Even then she turned toward the poor. In Tarnów she opened an after-school club for neglected children; in Lublin she cared for her students’ spiritual and material needs alongside their academic formation.

1969 – 1971 Athens · Turkey

A first mission among the poor

In 1969 she asked to go on the missions. Her superiors sent her to a monastery in Greece, and she spent two years working among the poor of Athens. She also travelled briefly to Turkey, where she carried on her pastoral work discreetly.

1971 – 1974 Prakashpalayam, India

India — a call that would not leave

Her longing to be a missionary kept growing. On 12 December 1971 she left for Prakashpalayam, India, to join Polish missionaries there.

She saw how much the poor families around her needed their faith strengthened, and she gave herself to informal catechesis in their homes. She received this as God’s call and obtained special permission to stay beyond her original year.

1974 – 1981 Mariapura, Karnataka

A new family begins

In 1974, with great heartache, she asked to be released from her Ursuline vows. She had discerned that her path lay elsewhere. A group of young women was already gathering around her, drawn to the same calling.

In 1976, with their bishop’s consent, they decided to live together. On 5 May 1977 they moved into their first house in Mariapura — the date the congregation counts as its beginning. The Church approved temporary Constitutions on 11 November 1980, and Mother Regina made vows in the new congregation in 1981.

The early years Poverty and the Eucharist

Among the poorest

From the start, the sisters worked among the poorest and lived in hard conditions. Through patience and perseverance, Mother Regina won the trust of the people.

The Eucharist was the source of her strength. She often walked many kilometres so that she could attend Mass every day — a practice her sisters inherited from her.

2003 – 2012 Poznań-Pokrzywno, Poland

Return and rest

She never forgot her Ursuline roots and kept contact with the Ursulines of the Roman Union throughout her life. During a visit to Poland in 2003 she fell ill and understood it was time to set down her responsibilities.

She asked to stay with the Ursuline community in Poznań-Pokrzywno, and she died there on 23 September 2012, surrounded by sisters from both of her religious families.

“My path was, however, serving the poor.”

— Mother Regina, looking back on her life

Her work continues

Her spiritual daughters carry on her mission. Today the Satyaseva Sisters serve in dioceses across India — including Bangalore, Delhi, Agra, Mysore, Varanasi, Bareilly and Assam — and in Germany and Poland, the homeland of their foundress.
