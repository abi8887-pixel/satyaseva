Methodology

Sources & Limitations

Every source used on this site, and an honest account of what could not be verified.

Approach

This site was compiled entirely from publicly accessible sources: the congregation’s own website, the website of the Ursulines of the Roman Union (the order the founder belonged to before founding this congregation), Indian diocesan and archdiocesan sister directories, the Conference of Religious India member directory, and one independent journalism feature. Facts that could not be corroborated in at least one such source are left out rather than presented as established.

This congregation is small and diocesan-right, and its public documentary footprint is correspondingly modest. There is no independent biography, scholarly history, or dedicated encyclopedia article about it at the time of writing. Reported figures for total membership and community count vary slightly by source and publication year — most plausibly reflecting ordinary growth over time rather than any inconsistency to resolve.

This site deliberately does not rely on informal or AI-generated material describing the congregation that circulates outside its own channels, since such material could not be independently verified and in places appears to include invented specifics not found in any primary or diocesan source.

What is not covered here

  • A complete, current roster of all communities, with founding dates for each
  • Independently verified membership totals more recent than the 2015 Ursuline publication
  • Named details of specific schools or welfare homes beyond the Bengaluru foster-care ministry
  • Any scholarly or academic treatment of the congregation’s history or impact
  • Photographs — no verified images of this specific congregation were located

For current, authoritative information, contact the congregation’s Generalate in Bengaluru directly, or consult the Conference of Religious India member directory.
